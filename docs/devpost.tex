\section{Elevator pitch}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Most sports games assume two hands, a controller, and a body that can mash
buttons for ninety minutes. FIFA4ALL does not. We turn a MacBook webcam into
the pad: look left/right/up/down to hold WASD, open your mouth to hold Space
(shoot), wink to hold L (pass). Those OS-level key holds go into Google
Chrome running Amazon Luna, so you are playing real EA Sports FC --- not a
toy soccer demo --- without touching a keyboard or a DualShock.

The first valid face is your neutral stance. Sit up, tap the orange RESET on
the \texttt{FIFA4ALL look axis} overlay, then play. If tracking is lost,
every key comes up. It is a VTHacks accessibility MVP: different bodies,
same pitch.

\section{About the project}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{What it is}

FIFA4ALL is an accessibility project for VTHacks. The long-term idea is that
the interface should adapt to the user's remaining mobility instead of
forcing the user to adapt to a controller. The hackathon build is the
smallest version of that idea that still looks like \emph{FIFA}: webcam face
tracking on a Mac, mapped to the simplified EA Sports FC keyboard layout,
injected into Amazon Luna in Chrome.

We specifically wanted the real game. A custom soccer prototype reads as a
computer-vision demo. EA Sports FC on Luna reads as: you are actually
playing FIFA with your face.

\subsection*{How you play}

Live capture opens only the built-in \textbf{MacBook Pro Camera} (FaceTime /
built-in). It refuses iPhone and Continuity Camera indexes so a phone in the
room cannot become the tracker.

From the repository root, after a Python 3.12 venv and
\texttt{pip install -r tracking/requirements.txt}:

\begin{verbatim}
MPLCONFIGDIR=.cache/matplotlib python -m tracking.live --preview
\end{verbatim}

Headless (same holds, no overlay):

\begin{verbatim}
MPLCONFIGDIR=.cache/matplotlib python -m tracking.live --no-preview
\end{verbatim}

Open Chrome, launch Luna, start EA FC, and \textbf{click the game} so it is
focused. Quartz HID events
(\texttt{CGEventPost}) land on whatever is frontmost.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item[Look / head (nose joystick)]
    WASD holds. The first valid nose point is center; returning to center
    releases movement.
  \item[Mouth open]
    Space hold --- shoot for as long as the mouth stays open.
  \item[Wink]
    L hold --- pass while the wink is detected.
\end{description}

The preview window is titled \texttt{FIFA4ALL look axis}. Sit straight and
tap the orange \textbf{RESET} control to recapture a neutral pose. macOS
must grant \textbf{Camera} and \textbf{Accessibility} to Terminal / Python
or the camera will not open and key holds may be dropped.

Exclusive fullscreen covers the overlay; press Esc to leave it if you need
the HUD or RESET.

\subsection*{Why this mapping}

EA Sports FC on Luna can be played with a tiny keyboard set: move, shoot,
pass. That let us skip virtual gamepads and the browser Gamepad API. MediaPipe
Face Mesh (not a separate Tasks landmarker) measures a nose look-axis, mouth
opening, and an eye-wink signal; the live module holds the matching keys until
the gesture ends. Combinations work (look while shooting). Face lost means
all keys released --- safer than a stuck W into the advertising boards.

\subsection*{Who it is for}

Anyone who can sit in view of a laptop camera and move their head, mouth, or
eyes, but who cannot --- or would rather not --- use two analog sticks. It is
not yet a per-user calibration product and it is not a medical device. It is
a working hands-free path into a game people already recognize, built in a
weekend so more people can share the same pitch.
